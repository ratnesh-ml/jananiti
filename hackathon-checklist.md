# Code for Communities 2.0: Jananiti Submission Readiness

## Registration and Team Evidence

The publicly linked registration guide requires participants to register through Hack2Skill, save a Roadmap screenshot, form or join one team, save a complete Team page screenshot, select a track, and retain proof for the subsequent KonfHub step.[1] The KonfHub event listing says that a combined registration, team, and problem-statement proof must be uploaded during the required KonfHub registration.[2]

| Checklist item | Evidence to retain | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| Register each participant on Hack2Skill. | Roadmap screenshot for each required participant. | Requires team action in the Hack2Skill account. |
| Create or join the **8-BITS** team. | Team page screenshot showing the complete roster. | Requires team action in the Hack2Skill account. |
| Select the governance/public-infrastructure track. | Selected problem statement screenshot. | Jananiti aligns to AI for Digital Public Infrastructure & Governance. |
| Merge the required proof screenshots. | One clear PDF or image containing the required evidence. | Prepare after registration is complete. |
| Complete any required KonfHub registration and ticket process. | Confirmation or ticket email. | Follow the organizer’s current instructions. |

## Demo Storyline

| Demo beat | What judges see | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A citizen reports a blocked drain or damaged streetlight. | A short, structured report with category, location pin, privacy-safe visibility, and a clear submission acknowledgement. | Demonstrates accessible digital public infrastructure. |
| 2. Jananiti produces a reviewable triage draft. | Gemini-assisted summary/category/urgency is visible as a suggestion, not an opaque final decision. | Demonstrates responsible AI rather than surface-level automation. |
| 3. A coordinator opens the operations queue. | Role-gated review, assignment, status management, and a progress note appear in one workspace. | Demonstrates a credible government operations workflow. |
| 4. The citizen tracks progress. | Personal timeline and public-safe status update show receipt, ownership, work in progress, and resolution. | Demonstrates accountability and closes the feedback loop. |
| 5. The geographic activity view highlights local patterns. | Google Maps shows issue location and area-level activity. | Demonstrates an immediately useful Google technology integration and a path to prioritization. |

## Differentiators to State Precisely

Jananiti is differentiated by its **auditable report-to-resolution workflow**, **map-native citizen participation**, and **human-reviewed AI triage**. The product is intentionally designed to grow toward the supplied solution’s multi-channel, multilingual, and data-fusion vision without pretending that those advanced services are already live.

The publicly available participant materials reviewed so far describe registration and attendance steps but do **not** surface a formal judging rubric. The team should obtain the rubric from the official Hack2Skill portal or organizer before final submission, then revise this checklist around its exact scoring dimensions. Until then, the build prioritizes the public materials’ evident themes: meaningful governance impact, practical AI, Google technology relevance, usability, and a credible path to scale.[2][3]
